"Ultimate Guide: How to Build a Killer, ATS-Friendly Resume Using AI & LaTeX

July 3, 2026
In today’s hyper-competitive job market, applying for a job isn't just about impressing a human recruiter anymore. Before a hiring manager ever lays eyes on your application, your resume has to pass through a digital gatekeeper: the Applicant Tracking System (ATS).

Did you know that recruiters spend an average of only 6 to 8 seconds scanning a resume? But even more shocking is that up to 75% of resumes are filtered out by ATS bots before a human ever sees them!

If you are using fancy templates with multi-column layouts, graphics, tables, progress bars, or text boxes, you are likely setting yourself up for automatic rejection. The ATS bots simply cannot parse these formats, leading to garbled data and an instant "no".

Fortunately, you can use the power of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and LaTeX to build a modern, high-impact, single-page resume that is 100% readable by ATS algorithms and visually stunning to recruiters.

In this comprehensive guide by Next Techy Pixel by Mondal Products, we will walk you through a complete, step-by-step workflow to audit, rewrite, and generate an elite tech resume.

Article content
1. Header

Keep it clean. Your name should be the largest text on the page, followed by your target job title. Include your location (City, State/Country), phone number, email address, and essential hyperlinks: GitHub, LinkedIn, and your Portfolio.

Avoid: Hyperlinking words like "My Portfolio." Write out the clean link text (e.g., github.com/yourprofile) because some older ATS parsers strip away hyperlink formatting, leaving the text unclickable and empty.

2. Professional Summary

A concise, 3 to 4-line hook that establishes who you are, your top skills, the value you bring, and your career direction.

3. Technical Skills

Group your skills logically. Instead of a messy block of keywords, break them down into:

Programming Languages
Web & Mobile Development Frameworks
Cloud & DevOps Tools
Database Systems & Core Computer Science Concepts

4. Professional Experience

List your roles in reverse chronological order (newest first). Focus on achievements rather than a list of daily duties. State the Company, Location, Job Title, and Dates (formatted consistently as MMM YYYY – MMM YYYY or Present).

5. Projects

If you are a student or a fresher, your projects are your currency. Showcase 2 to 3 major projects, explicitly calling out the tech stack used and providing GitHub links or live demo links.

6. Education

List your degree, major, CGPA/percentage, institution name, location, and graduation date.

7. Achievements & Certifications

Add credibility with competitive achievements, hackathon placements, and certifications from recognized bodies (like AWS, Google, or NPTEL).

🎯 The Secret Experience Bullet Formula

The biggest mistake job seekers make is writing bullet points that look like a job description.

Weak: "Responsible for managing the frontend and fixing bugs."

Instead, use Next Techy Pixel's Experience Bullet Formula:

$$\text{Action Verb} + \text{Task/Tech Stack} + \text{Measurable Impact}$$

Let’s look at some powerful examples:

MERN Stack / Frontend Developer Example:Before: Worked on React apps and optimized web page speed.After: “Developed a responsive MERN stack web application that improved user experience by 40% and reduced page load times by 30%.”
Cloud & DevOps Example:Before: Set up CI/CD pipelines using GitHub Actions.After: “Automated CI/CD pipelines using GitHub Actions, reducing software deployment time by 50% across production environments.”

💡 Pro Tip: Always quantify your achievements with numbers, percentages, and metrics. It gives immediate credibility and scale to your work!
🛠️ The Complete Step-by-Step AI-to-LaTeX Workflow

While tools like Microsoft Word or basic online resume builders are common, LaTeX is the gold standard for tech resumes. LaTeX outputs clean, highly structured, vector-based PDF files. When an ATS parser reads a LaTeX-generated PDF, the text hierarchy is crystal clear.

Here is the exact step-by-step workflow to generate a professional LaTeX resume using AI and a "secret" free website:

Step 1: Paste the Prompt & Attach Your Current Details

Open ChatGPT or Claude. Paste your master prompt (found in our cheat sheet below). Click the "+" button, attach your existing resume PDF or paste your raw academic and experience details, and click Enter.

Step 2: ChatGPT Generates and You Copy the LaTeX Code

The AI will generate highly optimized raw LaTeX code. Do not worry if you don’t know how to code in LaTeX! All you need to do is go to the top right of the generated code box in ChatGPT or Claude and click Copy (or use Ctrl + C).

Step 3: Paste Your Code onto Overleaf (The Secret Website)

Go to Overleaf.com (a free, web-based collaborative LaTeX editor).

Create a free account and start a Blank Project.
Open the main.tex file.
Select all the existing default template code and delete it.
Paste your copied code from the AI using Ctrl + V in the editor window.

Final Step: Recompile and Export as PDF

Click the green Recompile button (or press Ctrl + Enter on your keyboard). Watch the magic happen on the right side of the screen as your perfectly formatted, professional resume compiles! Review the design, click the Download PDF icon, and you are ready to apply.

1. Resume Audit (Gap Analysis)

"Audit my resume against this job description below. Compare both and tell me exactly where my resume is failing. Which keywords are missing? What skills or requirements are they looking for that I have not mentioned? Give me a clear gap analysis so I know exactly what needs to change."
2. Rewrite for ATS Optimization

"Rewrite my resume to score as high as possible on ATS systems. Naturally inject the missing keywords from the job description into my experience and skills sections. Do not stuff them awkwardly; make it read like a real human wrote it. Keep all my actual experience accurate, but reframe and reword it to match what the job is asking for. Show me the before and after for each section you change."
3. Experience Bullet Rewrite

"Rewrite my current work experience bullet points using strong action verbs, measurable results, ownership, business impact, and technical relevance. Remove weak wording and make every single line look highly valuable using the Action Verb + Task + Impact formula."
4. Technical Skills Showcase

"Create a targeted skills section that highlights my most relevant hard skills, soft skills, and technical expertise for a [Job Role]. Group them logically (e.g., Programming Languages, Developer Tools, Frameworks) and align them with the job description keywords."
5. Freshers Resume Builder

"I am a fresher with no full-time experience. Build me a strong resume using my projects, internships, certifications, skills, coursework, and strengths so that I look interview-worthy, capable, and highly motivated."
6. The Final ATS Checklist Run

"Run a final ATS check on my resume. Look for formatting issues, missing keywords, readability problems, or anything that could cause an ATS to reject or misread it. Give me a checklist of fixes and what exactly to change."
📑 The Ultimate ATS Optimization Checklist

Use this handy checklist to ensure your resume remains "machine-readable" and highly appealing:

Article content
✅ DO THIS:

[ ] Use standard fonts: Stick to Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman, or Helvetica.
[ ] Font sizes: Body text should be 10–12pt; headings should be 12–14pt.
[ ] Use clear section headings: Write "EDUCATION", "TECHNICAL SKILLS", and "PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE". Don't try to be clever with headings like "Where I Learned" or "My Professional Journey".
[ ] Save as PDF: Unless explicitly asked for a .docx file, always export and upload your LaTeX resume as a PDF.
[ ] Keep it to 1 Page: If you have less than 5 years of experience, keep everything on a single, tightly edited page.
[ ] Proofread multiple times: Ensure zero grammatical or spelling errors.

❌ AVOID THIS:

[ ] No tables, columns, or charts: ATS parsers read from left to right; tables and multi-column designs break this parsing order.
[ ] No text boxes, graphics, or logos: These elements look like blank spaces to ATS algorithms.
[ ] No photos or profile pictures: Many countries reject resumes with photos immediately to comply with anti-discrimination hiring laws.
[ ] No long paragraphs: Never write long blocks of text; stick strictly to bullet points.
[ ] No fancy fonts or icons: Avoid non-standard bullet points or symbols.
